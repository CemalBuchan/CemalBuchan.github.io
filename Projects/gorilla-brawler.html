Arena Survival · Unity / C#

Gorilla vs 100

An arena survival prototype inspired by the viral "100 Humans vs 1 Gorilla" phenomenon. The player controls a single gorilla, fighting off endless waves of enemies while trying to survive as long as possible and achieve the highest score.

Unity C# Enemy AI Procedural Generation
Play on itch.io ↗

Overview

Developed over roughly 1.5–2 months, this project was less a polished game and more a design and technical exploration prototype — I focused on unique combat mechanics, player feedback systems, dynamic AI behavior, and performance optimization.

Each wave spawns 50 enemies that relentlessly pursue the player — the meme's original 100 was scaled down to 50 for performance stability without sacrificing the pressure of an overwhelming crowd. To keep visual variety high, enemies are procedurally generated from random combinations of 4 character models, 2 material variations, and 3 walking animations, creating a lively battlefield with minimal asset overhead.

Systems I built

  • Sensory Organ System — instead of a traditional health bar, the gorilla has four independent sensory organs (both eyes, nose, ears); destroying each one triggers a real gameplay effect — half the screen going dark, enemies no longer staying constantly visible, the special-attack warning glow disappearing, and so on.
  • Enemy AI — enemies can dodge poorly-timed attacks, and some perform a three-phase special attack (pause → charge → leap); this attack can be countered with a well-timed block, otherwise it damages a specific sensory organ.
  • Procedural Enemy Variation — a variation system that randomly combines models, materials, and animations to keep 50+ enemies on screen at once without a performance hit.
  • Player Abilities & Crowd Control — movement, sprint, melee attack, block, and a middle-click special ability with area damage; enemies launched into the air collide with those behind them, creating chain damage.
  • Visual Feedback System — color-coded cues (yellow glow for an incoming special attack, red glow for attacking/damage, white glow for temporary visibility after losing both eyes) that communicate instantly without relying on UI.
  • Performance Optimization — optimization work to keep 50+ active enemies running smoothly on screen at the same time.

What I took away from this project

This was one of the projects that pushed my growth the most in performance optimization, AI behavior design, animation event systems, and sound design. Keeping 50+ enemies on screen at once forced me into serious performance optimization; designing AI behaviors like dodging and special attacks deepened my understanding of AI; I learned to precisely sync attack timing (windup, hit, damage) through animation events; and layering all of these systems with sound taught me the value of giving the player clear, instant feedback.
